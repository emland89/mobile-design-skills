# Hierarchy and composition

- Start with content priority, not containers.
- Establish a dominant reading path using scale, placement, type, contrast, grouping, and whitespace.
- Equal visual weight implies equal importance; do not make every region loud.
- Use alignment and repetition to establish rhythm, then break rhythm only to communicate meaningful priority.
- Let high-value content occupy space proportionally to its importance/frequency.
- Avoid cardifying every group. Separation can come from spacing, dividers, typography, background shifts, or native grouping.
- Do not use whitespace as an excuse to hide useful information or force scrolling.
- On large windows, constrain reading widths and create meaningful panes/regions rather than enlarging everything.
- Test the hierarchy blurred or in grayscale: the dominant and secondary regions should still be apparent.
