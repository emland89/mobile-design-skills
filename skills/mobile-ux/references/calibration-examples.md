# UX calibration examples

These are decision examples, not screen templates. Use them when a request risks collapsing form factors or platforms into one interface.

## Watch feature admission

**Input:** A meal-planning phone app has recipe search, pantry management, weekly plan editing, grocery checklist, cooking timers, and nutrition reports.

**Good watch decision:** keep grocery check-off, cooking timers, time-sensitive reminders, and perhaps a glanceable next-meal complication. Consider omitting recipe discovery, pantry bulk editing, weekly planning, and nutrition-report analysis unless a concrete wrist use case exists.

**Why:** feature selection follows wrist advantage, not phone parity.

## iPhone to iPad

**Input:** A mail-like app has inbox -> message drill-down on iPhone.

**Good tablet decision:** preserve the conceptual hierarchy but evaluate simultaneous list/detail, sidebar destinations, keyboard shortcuts, pointer states, and narrow-window fallback. Do not add panes unless they reduce navigation or comparison cost.

## Desktop to mobile

**Input:** A desktop admin tool has dense tables, hover controls, sidebars, and 20 commands.

**Good mobile decision:** identify the few mobile jobs, replace hover-only affordances, prioritize commands by context, and redesign information access around interruption/touch rather than shrinking the desktop chrome.

## iOS to Android

**Input:** Product wants identical navigation for brand consistency.

**Good decision:** preserve product concepts and destination relationships while allowing platform-native back, modality, system surfaces, and navigation expression.
